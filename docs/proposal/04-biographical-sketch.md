# Biographical Sketch

[Author Name] began their formal encounter with theoretical physics at the age of twelve,
working through Russell Serway's *Physics for Scientists and Engineers* — their mother's
college textbook — during summers that most children spent outside. By fifteen they had
graduated high school. The precocity was real; so were the circumstances that made the
next decade difficult. They spent nearly ten years in college, interrupted repeatedly by
conditions that made conventional completion effectively impossible, and eventually made a
deliberate choice: a personal library and self-directed study rather than a credential the
available institutions were poorly positioned to grant.

That choice is not incidental to the argument of this book. *The Logic of Logos* contends
that the inherited formalizations of science and mathematics carry assumptions that
specialists are often least positioned to question, because specialization and the
assumptions are acquired together. An autodidact crosses the disciplinary lines along which
those assumptions run. The intellectual biography and the intellectual argument are the same
argument.

The formalization problem — the central problem of this book — first presented itself not
in a seminar room but in their teenage study of nuclear and subatomic particle physics. The
mass-energy accounting of the neutron and proton, and the formalism of annihilation and
creation operators, raised an early and persistent suspicion: the incompatibility between
general relativity and quantum mechanics might not be a contradiction awaiting physical
resolution but something more structurally intractable. If resolving the incompatibility
required internalizing a contradiction into the object language of physics, then no
classical or consistent theory could accomplish the unification, regardless of the equations
tried.

The hypothesis sharpened in the early 2000s through participation in Hypography — a
technically serious online science community. It was there, examining the positron's
discovery as a case study in what might be called logic-method agnosticism, that the core
claim crystallized: the GR–QM incompatibility is simultaneously a physical contradiction
*and* a formalization problem. These are not mutually exclusive. The same period introduced
them to the Brazilian school of paraconsistency — Newton da Costa's $C_n$ hierarchy — as a
framework for reasoning about matter–antimatter theories without trivializing the
contradiction they encode. Discussions migrated from Hypography to Twitter, where they were
an early adopter and critically live-tweeted the 2011 Singularity Summit, engaging with and
challenging the assumptions about computability and mind that inform both the simulation
hypothesis and the mathematical universe hypothesis — positions to which they have developed
what they call reasonable negative answers.

Their most important intellectual collaborations have been with three people. Dr. Damien
Williams, encountered on Twitter and later a Ph.D. in Science, Technology, and Society,
developed with them the metalinguistic argument at the core of the impossibility thesis:
what it means for two theories to be genuinely incomparable rather than merely
inconsistent. Dr. Paola Zizzi, an Italian physicist and logician, was their guide through
the foundations of qubit logics over a twelve-year correspondence; Zizzi's work on qubit
spacetime remains the single most important secondary source in this book. James T. Martin,
a programmer and logician encountered in programming-language design discussions, was their
collaborator from 2021 to 2024 on a project they called the Desert of the Surreal — a joint
effort that produced the theorem on functional incompleteness of non-degenerate subclassical,
substructural, and non-classical logics, along with the commutative diagrams of the network
of logical extensions that now form the structural backbone of *The Logic of Logos*.

They helped found the game design curriculum at Butte Community College, teaching the first
three semesters of Introduction to Game Design under Boyd Trolinger. In 2014, they presented
a preliminary version of the logos-variable framework at the 5th World Congress on
Paraconsistency, held at the Indian Statistical Institute in Kolkata — the campus of the
Einstein–Tagore dialogues. A two-part paper developing that presentation into a formal
impossibility theorem was subsequently withdrawn: not because the argument was wrong, but
because it required formal preliminaries that did not yet exist in a form shareable across
the six disciplines the argument addresses. *The Logic of Logos* is those preliminaries.
It is the book that makes the papers possible.
